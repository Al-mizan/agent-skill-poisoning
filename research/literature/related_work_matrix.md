# Related Work Matrix and Research Gap Synthesis

This matrix synthesizes verified primary literature on AI agent skill supply-chain security, cross-lingual safety alignment disparities, and runtime policy enforcement. All author names, publication years, and identifiers have been verified against arXiv or publisher records. Fields not explicitly reported in the accessed primary sources are marked `TBD`.

## 2. Verified Research Gaps

Cross-referencing the 16 primary studies above establishes four concrete gaps that our research addresses:

1. **Systematic Exclusion of Non-English Skills in Runtime Benchmarks**: Every existing active skill-poisoning benchmark, including **Skill-Inject** (Schmotz et al., 2026), **MalSkillBench** (Guo et al., 2026), **Under the Hood of SKILL.md** (Saha et al., 2026), **SkillJect** (Jia et al., 2026), and **DDIPE** (Qu et al., 2026), operates exclusively in English or explicitly filters out non-English `SKILL.md` files during dataset construction.
2. **Static Observation Without Active Runtime Exploitation**: Zhang, Yuan, et al. (2026) proved that static scanners exhibit a severe **English Calibration Gap**, flagging non-English skills 3 to 5 times more often for behavioral anomalies while achieving 0% precision on Japanese samples. However, their work never executed a single skill inside an LLM agent. Whether non-English or code-mixed **Contextual Injection** inside `SKILL.md` increases runtime **Skill Selection Rate (SSR)** and **Attack Success Rate (ASR)** remains unmeasured.
3. **Absence of Code-Mixed South Asian Registers in Agentic Tool Use**: While **BanglaVeilGuard** (Hassan & Hossain, 2026) and **MIPIAD** (Muhtadi & Tazwar, 2026) examine Bangla in direct chatbot jailbreaks and static BIPIA text classification, no study tests native-script Bangla or colloquial Banglish code-mixing inside **Agent Skill** manifests where English CLI commands and tool schemas coexist with non-English natural-language instructions.
4. **Language Invariance of Deterministic vs. Semantic Defenses**: White-box probing defenses such as Dong et al. (2026) require hidden-state access unavailable in commercial API agents, while spec-translation monitors such as **VIGIL** (Li, Chen, et al., 2026) and static scanners depend on natural-language parsing. Evaluating whether an in-process **Policy Enforcement Module** at the `dispatch()` execution seam (per [0002-deterministic-runtime-policy-enforcement-seam.md](file:///home/almizan/Other%20Locations/workspace/research/docs/adr/0002-deterministic-runtime-policy-enforcement-seam.md)) provides language-invariant protection without the over-refusal tax documented by Hassan & Hossain (2026) provides a clear defensive contribution.
